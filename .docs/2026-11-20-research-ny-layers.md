# New York: how its local law is layered

Step 1 of the `state-offices` skill for New York. Plan: `plans/2026-11-20-new-york-state-offices.md`.
Statute pages are the NY Senate's text, read 2026-11-20; each page's "most recent revision" date is noted where it
is later than 2014-09-22.

## Ranking: constitution, general law, charter, local law

The constitution gives every local government power to adopt local laws, bounded by the constitution and general
law. [N.Y. Const. Art. IX § 2(c)(ii)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNS/A9S2):

> every local government shall have power to adopt and amend local laws not inconsistent with the provisions of
> this constitution or any general law relating to the following subjects,

and the first subject, § 2(c)(ii)(1), is its officers:

> The powers, duties, qualifications, number, mode of selection and removal, terms of office, compensation, hours
> of work, protection, welfare and safety of its officers and employees, except that cities and towns shall not
> have such power with respect to members of the legislative body of the county in their capacities as county
> officers.

"Local government" is "A county, city, town or village"
([Art. IX § 3(d)(2)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNS/A9S3)). A "general law" applies "alike to all
counties, all counties other than those wholly included within a city, all cities, all towns or all villages"; a
"special law" to "one or more, but not all" of them (§ 3(d)(1), (4); the same in
[MHRL § 2(5), (12)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/MHR/2)).

The Municipal Home Rule Law carries this out. [MHRL § 10(1)(ii)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/MHR/10)
(revision 2021-10-29) repeats the grant and the officers subject in § 10(1)(ii)(a)(1), which "shall include but
not be limited to the creation or discontinuance of departments of its government".

A "charter" is "A state statute or a local law which establishes or continues a specific county, city or village
as a municipal corporation" (MHRL § 2). Towns are not in that list: a town has no charter.

**Local law can supersede the town and village general laws.** This is the layer that matters most here: the
general law is a default each town and village may change for itself.

- Towns, § 10(1)(ii)(d)(3): may amend or supersede a Town Law provision "notwithstanding that such provision is a
  general law, unless the legislature expressly shall have prohibited the adoption of such a local law", except
  (without other authority) on special or improvement districts, taxation areas, referendum, and "town finances".
- Villages, § 10(1)(ii)(e)(3): "The amendment or supersession in its application to it, of any provision of the
  village law relating to the property, affairs or government of the village ... notwithstanding that such
  provision is a general law, unless the legislature expressly shall have prohibited the adoption of such a local
  law."

Limits, [MHRL § 11](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/MHR/11) (revision 2019-01-11): no local law may
supersede a state statute that "Applies to or affects the courts" (§ 11(1)(e)) or the comptroller's audit powers
(§ 11(1)(g)), and a county, city or village may not amend its charter "contrary to any provisions of such charter
regulating its own amendment" (§ 11(2)(a)).

## What a charter's silence means

**Counties: the County Law fills it.** [County Law § 2](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNT/2): "This
chapter shall apply to all counties," except (a) "a county wholly contained within a city, unless specifically so
provided", and (b) its provisions "in so far as they are in conflict with or in limitation of a provision of any
alternative form of county government ... shall not be applicable to the county, unless a contrary intent is
expressly stated in this chapter." Only conflicting provisions are displaced, so where a charter says nothing, the
County Law applies. (Reading of § 2(b); no section states the silent case in words.) Same answer as Vermont and
Maine, unlike Oregon.

**Towns and villages: no charter to be silent.** Every town runs under the Town Law and every village under the
Village Law, each as changed by its own local laws (above). A village that kept a special-act charter would be the
exception: not yet confirmed whether any remain (open, below).

**Cities: each city's charter.** No general-law form of city government was found. The General City Law grants
powers "Subject to the constitution and general laws of this state" to "every city"
([GCT § 20](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GCT/20), revision 2019-01-11), including "To determine and
regulate the number, mode of selection, terms of employment, qualifications, powers and duties" of officers
(§ 20(17)); it sets no officers. Charters are revised by local law
([MHRL § 36](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/MHR/36), revision 2026-05-29).

The one statute that sets city officers is the Second Class Cities Law, and it applies only to a city "which on
the thirty-first day of December, nineteen hundred and twenty-three was a city of the second class", "until such
provision is superseded pursuant to the municipal home rule law"
([SCC § 4](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/SCC/4)). For those cities it provides "a mayor, comptroller,
treasurer, president of the common council and four assessors" and ward aldermen
([SCC § 11](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/SCC/11)), with legislative power "vested in the common
council" ([SCC § 30](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/SCC/30)). Which cities that still reaches is open.

## Which places run under general law

- **Towns** (933 in open-data): the Town Law, by class.
  [TWN § 10](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/10) (revision 2023-11-26): first class, "Towns
  containing a population of ten thousand or more" and every Westchester town; second class, "less than ten
  thousand", with some counties' towns assigned by name. Both classes have a supervisor and town council members
  ([TWN § 20](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/20), revision 2022-09-02), and "the supervisor and the
  town council members shall constitute the town board" ([TWN § 60](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/60),
  revision 2022-08-19). A town of 25,000, or 7,500 near a large city, may adopt the suburban town law by
  resolution and referendum ([TWN § 50-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/50-A)), in which "The
  supervisor shall be the chief executive officer" ([TWN § 52](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/52)).
  The class can't be read from the name; a population threshold or a county decides it, and suburban status only
  from the Secretary of State's list (§ 50-a(5)(b)).
- **Villages** (531): the Village Law. "Every village shall have" a mayor, four trustees (the board may change the
  number), a treasurer and a clerk; "The mayor, trustees and village justices shall be elective officers"; "The
  mayor and the trustees of a village shall constitute the board of trustees"
  ([VIL § 3-301](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VIL/3-301), revision 2016-08-26).
- **Cities** (62): no general law; see above.
- **Counties** (57 outside New York City): the County Law unless a charter. The board of supervisors is "The
  supervisors of the several cities and towns in each county"
  ([CNT § 150](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNT/150)), or "an elected county legislative body, by
  whatsoever name designated" in its place ([CNT § 150-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNT/150-A)). A
  county may adopt a charter ([N.Y. Const. Art. IX § 1(h)(1)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CNS/A9S1);
  [MHRL § 33](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/MHR/33)), which "shall set forth the structure of the county
  government" and may "Empower an executive officer elected on a county-wide basis to veto actions of the board"
  (§ 33(2), (4)(b)).
- **New York City's five counties and boroughs**: the County Law does not apply to "a county wholly contained
  within a city" (CNT § 2(a)), and the county charter article excludes "a county in the city of New York"
  ([MHRL § 32(3)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/MHR/32)). They have no county government of their own;
  the city's charter governs. The 5 county and 5 borough records in open-data are not jurisdictions to collect.

### Law that binds a chartered or local-law place regardless

Recorded for a later config field.

- Art. IX § 2(c)(ii)(1) and MHRL § 10(1)(ii)(a)(1): cities and towns have no power over "members of the
  legislative body of the county in their capacities as county officers" (the supervisor who sits on a county board
  of supervisors).
- MHRL § 11(1): no local law may supersede a statute on the courts or the comptroller's audits.
- MHRL § 34(3) (revision 2026-05-29): a county charter "shall not supersede any general or special law" on taxes,
  or "Insofar as it relates to a function, power or duty of the state or of any officer or agency thereof".
- MHRL § 33(2): a county charter keeps "an elective board of supervisors, the members of which shall be deemed
  county officers" (the legislature, under whatever name).
